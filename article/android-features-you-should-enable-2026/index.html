10 Android Features You Should Enable Right Now

Your Android phone can do much more than make calls and run apps. It also includes settings that can help protect your personal information, improve account security and make a lost device easier to manage.

Introduction

Your Android phone can do much more than make calls and run apps. It also includes settings that can help protect your personal information, improve account security and make a lost device easier to manage.

The exact menu names and features vary by manufacturer, Android version and region. Use the settings search box if a menu looks different on your phone.

1. Set a strong screen lock

Open Settings and look under Security, Security and privacy or Lock screen. Use a strong PIN or password and enable biometric unlocking if your device supports it.

Avoid easy-to-guess PINs such as repeated digits or birthdays. Remember that biometrics usually work alongside a PIN, password or pattern.

2. Review app permissions

Apps may request access to your camera, microphone, location, contacts and photos. Give each app only the permissions it needs.

Go to Settings and search for Permission manager or Privacy dashboard. Review access to sensitive permissions and revoke access that no longer makes sense.

Google explains the Privacy Dashboard in its Android Help documentation.

3. Keep Android and apps updated

Security updates can fix vulnerabilities. Check for Android system updates and install updates for apps through Google Play.

Menu paths differ, but you can usually find system updates under Settings → System → Software update, or a similarly named section. Restart when required.

4. Check Google Play Protect

Google Play Protect checks apps for potentially harmful behaviour. Make sure it is enabled in the Google Play Store settings and run a scan if your device offers that option.

It is an important layer of protection, not a guarantee that every harmful app or scam will be detected.

Google Play Protect and Android security

5. Turn on available theft-protection features

Search Settings for Theft protection, Find Hub, Find My Device or Device protection. Enable the options supported by your phone.

Depending on your device and Android version, available features may include theft detection, offline finding, remote locking or additional safeguards against unauthorised access.

Set these up before your phone goes missing. Some features require a Google Account, location settings, connectivity or prior configuration.

Android security and privacy

6. Secure your Google Account

Use a strong, unique password and enable two-step verification. Review devices signed in to your account and remove any you do not recognise.

If available, consider a passkey or other phishing-resistant sign-in method. Keep recovery information current so you can regain access if you lose your phone.

Google Account Security Checkup

7. Use a password manager

Avoid reusing the same password across email, banking, shopping and social accounts. A reputable password manager can help generate and store unique passwords.

Choose a trusted provider, protect its account with strong authentication and understand its recovery process. Do not store your master password in an unprotected note.

8. Review lock-screen notifications

Notifications can reveal message previews, one-time codes and personal details while your phone is locked.

Open notification or lock-screen settings and choose whether to hide sensitive content. The wording varies by device; search Settings for lock screen notifications.

9. Check backup settings

Confirm that important photos, contacts and other supported data are backed up. Check what is included, which account is used and whether storage is available.

A backup is useful only if it contains the information you need and you can access the account that stores it. Keep a separate copy of especially important files where appropriate.

10. Turn on safer browsing and messaging protections

Use a supported browser's safe-browsing protection and review spam or scam-filtering features in your messaging app. Google Messages and Phone by Google may offer protections on supported devices and in supported regions.

Treat unexpected links, payment requests and urgent messages cautiously—even if the sender appears familiar.

Android security features

Five-minute Android security checklist

  • Screen lock is strong and current.
  • Camera, microphone and location permissions have been reviewed.
  • Android and apps are updated.
  • Play Protect is enabled.
  • Available theft-protection and device-finding features are configured.
  • Google Account recovery and two-step verification are checked.
  • Lock-screen notifications do not expose sensitive details.
  • Important data is backed up.

Frequently asked questions

Do all Android phones have the same settings?

No. Menu names, feature support and rollout schedules vary by brand, model, Android version and region.

Does Play Protect guarantee that my phone is safe?

No. It can identify many known harmful apps and behaviours, but users should still install apps from trusted sources, review permissions and avoid suspicious links.

Should I leave location enabled?

Use location when it supports a feature you need, such as navigation or finding a lost device. Review which apps can access it and choose the least-permissive option that works for you.

Conclusion

You do not need to change every Android setting. Start with your screen lock, updates, app permissions, account security and device-finding features. These simple checks can improve your protection without making your phone difficult to use.
